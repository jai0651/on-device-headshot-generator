DML · under the hood

Models

Every network in the project: which ones are downloaded open-source weights and which ones we trained, what goes into each and what comes out, and exactly where our additions plug into Stable Diffusion.

Open-source vs trained by us

Almost everything is a pretrained open-source model used frozen. The project trains exactly two things: a tiny diffusion model in stage 0 (to learn how diffusion works), and a face LoRA, a 3.2M-parameter add-on that teaches Stable Diffusion one face. Everything else is either downloaded or built (code, not weights): the evaluation harness, the pose template, the dataset audit.

Why so little training

Training a text-to-image model from scratch costs millions of GPU-hours. What this project does is adapt one: the face LoRA touches 0.37% of the UNet's parameters, trains in 13 minutes, and leaves the other 99.63% exactly as Stability AI released them. Stage 5's FaceID goes further and trains nothing per person. That difference is the whole on-device question.

Licences matter before anything ships

Everything marked non-commercial (insightface's recognizers and IP-Adapter-FaceID, which is built on them) is fine for this study and not fine for a product. A shipping version needs a commercially licensed face encoder, and would have to retrain the FaceID adapter against it.

Inside the UNet: where everything plugs in

The UNet is where all the identity and layout work happens, so it's worth seeing its shape. It shrinks the 64×64 latent down to 8×8 and back up, with skip connections carrying detail across. At three of the four resolutions there are transformer blocks, 16 in total. Each transformer block has a self-attention layer (the image attending to itself) and a cross-attention layer (the image attending to the prompt). Those attention layers are the levers.

Zoom into one of those 16 transformer blocks and you can see where the two identity methods differ. Both work through attention, but at different points and with different inputs.

LoRA: change the weights, a little

Each of the 128 projections (16 blocks × 2 attentions × q/k/v/out) gets a low-rank update B·A. Rank 16 on a 320×320 projection is 10,240 new numbers instead of 102,400. Trained per person, so it holds this face very specifically, and it can be folded into W afterwards so it costs nothing at inference.

FaceID: add a second thing to look at

No weights change per person. A face-recognition embedding is turned into 4 tokens, and each cross-attention gets its own K/V projections for them. The result is summed with the text attention, scaled by s. Trained once by its authors on many faces; works on a new face instantly. The 17-photo version simply provides 17 × 4 = 68 face tokens.
