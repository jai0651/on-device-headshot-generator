An on-device headshot generator, from the inside

A selfie in, a professional headshot out, of you, on the phone, with nothing uploaded. These pages explain how it's being built and measured, without needing to read any code.

What this is

The product idea: take one or a few selfies and produce studio-quality headshots (clean backdrop, good lighting, proper framing) that are recognisably you, entirely on an iPhone. Today's AI headshot apps upload 10–20 photos and train a model per customer on a server. This project asks how much of that can happen with no upload and no per-user training.

It's being built in nine stages, each producing something measurable, on Stable Diffusion 1.5: small enough to eventually run on a phone, and the best-documented open image model. Every claim on these pages comes from a run that was actually executed.

Glossary

TermMeaning here
Diffusion modelA network trained to remove a little noise from an image. Run repeatedly from pure noise, a picture appears.
Stable Diffusion 1.5The open image model everything builds on: a text encoder, a UNet and a VAE, ~1.07B parameters in total.
LatentSD's compressed image space: 512×512×3 pixels ↔ 4×64×64 numbers (48× smaller). The loop runs here.
UNetThe 860M-parameter network that predicts the noise at each step. 95% of the compute.
VAEConverts between pixels and latents. Encoder used for training, decoder at the end of generation.
Timestep / stepsHow noisy the current latent is (999 = pure noise, 0 = clean). Generation walks down in 25 steps.
Sampler / schedulerThe numerical method that turns noise predictions into the next latent. We use DPM++ 2M Karras.
CFG (guidance)Running the UNet with and without the prompt and pushing away from "no prompt". Makes images obey; doubles the cost.
EmbeddingA vector of numbers that stands for something: a prompt (77×768) or a face (512).
Cosine similarityHow aligned two vectors are, from −1 to 1. Our identity score: ≥ 0.30 means "same person".
Cross-attentionThe UNet layers where the image "looks at" the prompt. Where identity methods act.
LoRAA small trainable add-on (B·A) to frozen weight matrices. Ours: 3.19M parameters, 6 MB, one face.
Trigger phraseohwx man: the words the LoRA binds your face to.
CheckpointA snapshot of the LoRA saved during training (every 250 steps), each scored to pick the best.
IP-Adapter-FaceIDA pretrained add-on that feeds a face-recognition embedding into the UNet through extra attention. No per-person training.
ControlNetA copy of the UNet's encoder that reads a pose skeleton and steers the layout.
Recognizer (ArcFace)A face-recognition network: face in, 512-d identity vector out. Two independent ones measure identity.
CLIP scoreHow well an image matches its prompt, from 0 to 100. Typical values are in the 20s.
Compliance13 objective ID-photo checks: head size, eye line, centring, pose, background, lighting, sharpness, eyes, mouth.
Fixed prompt set8 prompts × 3 frozen seeds that every method generates, so scores are comparable.
PodA GPU rented from RunPod by the minute, created for one job and destroyed after.
Core ML / ANEApple's on-device model format and its Neural Engine accelerator (stages 6–8).
