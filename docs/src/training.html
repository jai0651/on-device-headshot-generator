The core loss is identical in both. Take a clean image, add a known amount of Gaussian noise, ask the network to predict that noise, and penalise the squared error. Everything else is what that network is, what it's conditioned on, and what's allowed to change.

The captioning rule, and why it's the most important step

Caption what should stay changeable, and never describe the face. The shirt, background, lighting and expression get words, so the model ties them to those words. The face is the only thing present in every photo and named by nothing except ohwx man, so that's what the token absorbs. Leave the red tilak or the hanging sunglasses uncaptioned and they'd become part of "you". The trigger is ohwx rather than the usual sks because sks already means a rifle to SD 1.5.

One LoRA training step (on the GPU)

What the LoRA actually is, in numbers

The update

For a frozen weight W (d × k), train A (r × k) and B (d × r), and use W + (α/r)·B·A. With r = α = 16, the scale is 1.

A 320×320 projection is 102,400 frozen numbers. Its LoRA is 16×320 + 320×16 = 10,240 trainable ones.

Where, and how many

16 transformer blocks × 2 attentions (self, cross) × 4 projections (q, k, v, out) = 128 LoRA pairs, 256 tensors, 3.19M parameters. The text-encoder variant adds 12 layers × 4 = 48 more pairs, 1.18M parameters.

Picking a checkpoint: a measurement, not a vibe

Each checkpoint's 8 sample images are scored on the Mac for identity (two recognizers), prompt adherence (CLIP) and compliance. The rule: take the highest identity among checkpoints that still follow prompts at least 90% as well as the untrained model. For run 1 that was step 1500.

Run 1: identity (green) climbs from stranger to ~0.47 and levels off; prompt adherence (red) never collapses, so it isn't overcooked at 2000 steps.

What's frozen is the point

Nothing in the base model moves. That's why the LoRA can't forget how to draw a suit or a mountain, why 17 photos are enough, and why the result is a 6 MB file rather than a 2 GB model. It's also why it doesn't fit the on-device product: each user would still need a GPU run, which is the problem stage 5's zero-shot FaceID removes.
